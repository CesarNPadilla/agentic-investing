# Whole-account and portfolio risk decisions — v1.0.1

The current production whole-account daily-loss amount remains **UNSET / FAIL CLOSED**. The proposed sleeve daily loss, `min($20, 2% of positive start-of-day sleeve equity)`, is separate and cannot be copied into `max_daily_loss`. [Production decisions](examples/risk-policy-v101/production-risk-decisions.json) keep the whole-account amount `UNRESOLVED` and the human response `PENDING`.

For a future whole-account stop, the owner must decide an amount and measurement basis. Proposed *method*, not a proposed amount: use reconciled account-wide marked equity, including non-sleeve existing holdings; measure realized plus unrealized P&L net of fees; remove the effect of deposits and withdrawals; use a recorded start-of-day equity baseline in a selected timezone; verify every position mark, activity, open order, and account balance; check on each proposal, account event, fresh mark, and before any future submission. The owner must choose the timezone/reset boundary and an operational feed frequency. Missing or stale data blocks new entries. A breach would block entries, require review of pending orders and reconciliation, and follow an approved persistent kill-switch rule. Cancels, liquidation, and reset are not automatic. A human reset should require cause review, fresh reconciliation and an auditable role. Exceptions to a hard stop are not proposed. Until the amount and method are approved and enforced independently of sleeve stops, production execution is blocked.

The proposed 25% same-name and 75% aggregate limits apply only to `EXPERIMENTAL_SLEEVE_RISK`, using positive sleeve equity as denominator. They are **not** whole-portfolio limits. For `WHOLE_PORTFOLIO_RISK`, the owner must decide a gross-exposure maximum and denominator (account equity or another explicit basis), treatment of correlated names and sectors, per-security concentration, handling of existing breaches, and exceptions. Existing nonallowlisted holdings remain valid and are included in reports; no forced liquidation. An existing breach can trigger monitoring and block an increasing new buy after an approved rule, but cannot silently convert to unlimited purchasing power. Pending buy commitments must count; reducing/canceling them releases capacity only after verified broker status. All these controls remain unresolved, so affected implementation and any production path fail closed.
